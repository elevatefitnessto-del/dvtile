## Full prompt

```text
SCENE CONTEXT
A 15-second brand film for DV Tile (Don Valley Tile), a Toronto marble, natural stone and tile installer, executed as ONE single unbroken camera move. The film is a living tile layout drawing that progressively turns into a photoreal, finished primary ensuite. There are no cuts, no dissolves, no fades, no editorial wipes anywhere: every scene change is caused by a visible in-world event — the drawing's own linework turning to stone, slabs settling along plotted joints, a plotted overlay — happening inside the scene while the camera keeps moving. Drawing and reality are the same geometry: every plotted joint line lands exactly where its real stone joint appears, and every plotted vein lands exactly on its real vein.

WORLD / HERO
An invented primary ensuite in a Toronto home, referencing no real house: honed Calacatta marble floor in 120 × 120 cm slabs; book-matched Calacatta slab wall rising behind a freestanding carved stone tub; a curbless shower with a recessed niche lit by a warm LED strip; brushed brass fixtures; a tall black steel window with autumn maple branches outside. The signature line in both worlds is a single flowing bronze vein #B88A55 — an S-curve that crosses the joint grid of the floor and continues up the book-matched wall, matched across every slab. Accent color definition, locked for the entire film: bronze #B88A55 is a warm burnished metal-gold, never orange, never yellow, in every beat and under every lighting condition. Drawing world: deep verde drafting paper #182F2A, faint millimeter grid rgba(242,242,239,0.10), calacatta-white #F2F2EF linework and type, bronze #B88A55 vein line, dimension lines and accent words. The drawing layer is honest 2D CAD tile-layout language: crisp 1px joint lines, a centered joint grid, slab numbers in each tile, dimension arrows with ticks, a vein-flow arrow, a title-block stamp, zero glow.

FIRST FRAME
Deep verde drafting paper fills the entire frame, the faint millimeter grid breathing at low opacity. Nothing else is in frame. Frame center holds the exact spot where the bronze vein is about to plot.

FORMAT
16:9 horizontal, 30fps, 15.0 seconds, one continuous take.

OPTICS
84° diagonal field of view, classic wide coverage. Lens locked for the entire 15 seconds — one lens, one take. Gentle anamorphic character: horizontal squeeze, oval bokeh, slight barrel edge distortion. NO lens flares, NO light streaks, NO floating bokeh circles. All type, dimensions and title-block graphics stay inside the central 70% of frame.

CAMERA — ONE UNBROKEN MOVE
The camera begins static, plumb overhead and face-on to the drawing, like a rostrum camera over a drafting table. As the floor plan turns to stone it descends slowly toward the floor, then tilts up from plumb to eye height as the walls rise, settling into a slow forward glide past the tub toward the shower niche. It then reverses into one long continuous pull-back and slight rise that never stops until the final 1.0-second hold, carrying the proof overlay, the warm evening light and the final lockup. All motion eases out; every move blends into the next with curved velocity. No handheld, no shake, no speed ramps.

AUDIO
Instrumental score: soft felt piano over a low warm pad, building gently from 6s, resolving on the lockup. Synchronized effects only: a fine pen-on-paper scratch as lines plot (0–3.6s), a low stone settle "thock" each time a slab lands (3.6–6s), a quiet water trickle at the niche (8–9.5s), a single soft chime as the underline draws (14s). No narration, no voice, no lyrics.

ACTION TIMING — transitions are in-world events, never cuts
0.0–1.8s — On the verde paper a single bronze S-curve vein plots itself through frame center; around it the ensuite FLOOR PLAN draws at plot speed: room outline, tub and shower outlines as line symbols, then a centered 120 × 120 joint grid laid out from the room's centerline, slab numbers appearing in each square, a bronze vein-flow arrow following the curve across four slabs. A title stamp rotates into the bottom-right title block: "DV TILE — LAYOUT REV.01". The headline plots above the plan stroke by stroke, 400ms per line: "Drawn before we cut." — the word "cut." plotted in bronze.
SEAM A (no cut): small calacatta labels leader-line onto the plan while the camera starts to descend.
1.8–3.6s — Labels plot on with leader lines: "120 × 120 HONED CALACATTA" · "VEIN-MATCHED, FLOOR TO WALL". The joint grid holds; the bronze vein brightens slightly as if charged.
SEAM B (no cut): materialization begins inside the linework itself at the room's centerline — the drawing's own joint lines start turning to stone; there is never a screen-space boundary.
3.6–6.0s — THE LINE-TO-STONE LAYING: starting from the centerline, each square of the grid becomes a real honed Calacatta slab, settling into place along its own plotted joint lines with a soft weight, edges perfectly flush; the bronze vein line turns into real gold-grey veining that flows continuously across the joints, exactly matched slab to slab. Slabs lay outward in sequence, two or three per beat. Where the plan meets the wall line, the vein continues upward: the wall's elevation lines rise into a book-matched marble slab wall, the vein mirrored at the center seam. The tub and shower outlines extrude into the carved stone tub and the glass shower. The verde paper beyond the room photochemically brightens into warm interior light, blooming outward from the finished floor. The camera, descending, tilts up to eye height as the walls rise. Headline: "From line to stone." — "stone." in bronze.
SEAM C (no cut): the eye-height glide continues toward the shower.
6.0–8.0s — Eye-height glide past the tub toward the curbless shower: the recessed niche cut into the vein-matched slab wall, mitred corners, its warm LED strip switching on. A plotted label holds in the air beside it: "LIPPAGE UNDER 1 MM", with a bronze dimension arrow measuring a joint at the shower threshold.
8.0–9.6s — At the niche: a brushed brass rainhead begins to run; water sheets down the slab and into a long linear drain. One plotted label: "FLOOD-TESTED BEFORE TILE". The camera begins the long pull-back it will hold to the end.
9.6–11.4s — PROOF OVERLAY (no peel, no wipe): the pull-back continues uninterrupted; over the photoreal room, calacatta-white and bronze linework plots itself directly ON TOP of the built stone — every joint line landing exactly on its real joint, the bronze vein line landing exactly on its real vein across floor and wall, two bronze dimension arrows snapping across slab widths. For a beat the drawing and the room coexist as one object; then the lines sink into the stone they trace while the light warms toward evening. Headline: "Exactly as drawn." — "Exactly" in bronze.
11.4–13.2s — The pull-back opens to the full ensuite at evening: warm LED glow under the vanity reflected in the honed floor, autumn maples dark outside the steel window, the faint millimeter grid still softly visible on the window glass only, holding both worlds in one frame.
13.2–15.0s — The same pull-back recedes the room to frame center as it darkens to deep verde; the remaining linework converges into the four-tile DV Tile mark — four square tiles with the bronze vein running through them — and the wordmark draws on in calacatta white: "DV TILE", with "Don Valley Tile" in a fine bronze script beneath. Secondary line in title-block type: "Call or text 437-929-2563 · dvtile.ca". Final 1.0-second hold.

STYLE
Architectural Digest restraint, photoreal stone with true subsurface translucency and fine pitting, accurate brass and glass reflections. No people, no tools left in frame, no logos other than DV Tile, no extra text beyond the exact copy.
```
